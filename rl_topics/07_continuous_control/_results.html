The 5 training test games happened to start in easy positions (−96). Over 100 random starts all three average about −140, close to the best commonly reported for Pendulum (about −150 to −140). A start hanging straight down costs about −350 just to swing up, so 0 is impossible. On this easy task, the three methods tie.

One swing-up, from hanging straight down

Nobody told them to pump: one push of ±2 isn't strong enough to lift the pendulum directly, so they discovered rocking. DDPG's rattle is an honest flaw: its policy flips sharply near upright and nothing in the reward penalised it.

Is the critic right? (start-state value)

            step 1,000      step 5,000      step 15,000      really (discounted)

Early critics are badly wrong (0 vs about −455 really); they learn the true value from bootstrapped targets over thousands of updates. By the end DDPG's critic is somewhat optimistic (−75 vs −88), while TD3 and SAC, with their min of twin critics, are slightly pessimistic (−97 and −98). That's exactly the bias each design aims for: pessimism is safer than optimism.

In one state ("45° right, still") the actor's torque (+0.39) is not at the hill's top (−2.0). But look at the slope: +0.426, positive, so the hill is rising towards larger torque at that point. The critic's hill there is almost flat (−85.3 everywhere), so a small local bump decides the direction. Gradient-based actors only see the local slope, not the whole hill: they can get stuck on a small local peak. It doesn't matter much here; the state rarely occurs.

The chain rule with the trained networks (state: 45° right of top)

z = +0.1970          a = 2·tanh(z) = +0.3890        Q = −85.309
∂Q/∂a = +0.4261                    (slope of the critic's hill at that torque)
∂a/∂b = 2·(1 − tanh²(z)) = 1.9243  (how the torque moves if the actor's last bias b moves)
∂Q/∂b = +0.4261 × 1.9243 = +0.8200     autograd says +0.8200 ✓
→ gradient ASCENT nudges b up by lr × 0.82, raising the torque

SAC is least sure when hanging down (σ = 0.50): it doesn't matter much which way it starts pumping. It's most sure near the top (σ = 0.20), where precision matters.

SAC's curiosity dial α, tuned by itself: 0.999 at the start → 0.211 after 2,000 updates → 0.096 after 7,000 → 0.047 at the end. Early on it pays a lot for randomness (explore!); once it knows how to balance, it buys certainty instead.
