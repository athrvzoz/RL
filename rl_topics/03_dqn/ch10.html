Reference: perfect play = 500; a random player ≈ 22; the largest possible true Q with γ = 0.99 is about 100.

What each result teaches

  • No target network → total collapse. Q-values grew to about 20 million, when no true value can exceed 100. Each update raised Q(s, a), which raised the target max Q(s2), which raised Q again: the dog chasing its tail, faster and faster. The agent scored 9, worse than random. This is the "moving target" problem of chapter 06, in real numbers.
  • No replay buffer → very slow and fragile. Learning only from the move just made gave a mean test score of 31.9 during training; one seed finally jumped up near the end. Consecutive CartPole moves are too similar to teach a network well.
  • Full DQN works but wobbles, as in chapter 09: good versions appear (best 474.6 for seed 0), then get worse again.
  • Double DQN was the only variant to reach a perfect 500 (seed 0), and had the best final and best networks on average. Honestly, its Q-values were not lower here (56.7 vs 55.4): on a game as simple as CartPole the over-optimism of chapter 06 is small. Double DQN's advantage was measured mostly on harder games such as Atari.
  • 2 seeds is not much. RL results vary a lot from seed to seed (look at full DQN: 474.6 vs 256.5). Serious comparisons use 5–10+ seeds. Treat small differences here as hints, big ones (9 vs 190) as real.
